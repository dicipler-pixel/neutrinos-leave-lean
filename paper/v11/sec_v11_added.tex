\section{Predictions that failed, and negative results}\label{sec:failed}

Every pre-registered prediction or reading in this paper that failed, in one place:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Diffusive escape} (built after seeing McCormick's curve, free exponent $1.61\pm0.03$ instead of $3/2$): excluded by Tests~A and~B.
\item \textbf{History-blind rule}: excluded by Tests~B and~D.
\item \textbf{Memory form without a fitted weight}: missed its own pre-set bar ($0.103>0.10$). The fitted form fails the flux--shear Test~F by a factor of forty, with the wrong sign at $r=4$.
\item \textbf{Linear first maximum} as a predictor of $G$: largest error $0.50$.
\item \textbf{Escape-rate and sharp-wall readings}: 32 parameter-free combinations, all rejected. The one that came within $0.077$ was picked post hoc, and it predicts $0.513$ for Test~B against $0.317$.
\item \textbf{Connection energy as the controlling quantity}: fails on the sign at $r=1/4,4$.
\item \textbf{$\sqrt{5/4}=1.118$ as the link rate}: survived the first $r=1.25,0.8$ search only because two crashed runs were counted as dispersals. The crash-safe rerun gives $1.1128$ $[1.1124,1.1131]$, which excludes it.
\item \textbf{Grid convergence of the rate at level~2}: failed its pre-set line ($-0.0082$ against $0.005$). The rate $1.10\pm0.01$ is \emph{not converged}.
\item \textbf{Stage-fix expectation at $r=1$}: failed ($+3.8\times10^{-5}$ against $<2\times10^{-5}$).
\item \textbf{Door in free streaming} (P3): not passed.
\item \textbf{Recorder prediction~(i)} (Section~\ref{sec:mcc}): killed.
\item \textbf{Gramian suppression and rank predictions} (P2b, P2d) in the linear study of Section~\ref{sec:gramian}: killed; three non-binding expectations there, including the lean that the rate falling late keeps more, also failed.
\item \textbf{Gramian order at the kill line}: only three of six fixed-opacity pairs pass the $0.08$ line.
\item \textbf{Kept-state angle as the ruler of the link law}: negative (Section~\ref{sec:link}); the kept-sector Riesz projector was ill-posed past the onset, so the author's projector ruler was not tested (Section~\ref{sec:posed}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{One model.} Every threshold is a run of McCormick's spherically symmetric code with his relaxation-time collision term (six groups in Section~\ref{sec:mcc} only). The reference thresholds (fluid, instantaneous, Table~II, the $\alpha=0.3$ and $R_\nu=0.2$ references, and his multigroup archive) are inputs from his archive and were not re-measured, except his instantaneous threshold at $T_H=1$\,MeV with the full equation of state, $0.53313\pm0.00012$.
\item \textbf{Formal link ratios.} The pairs outside $\tfrac34\le r\le\tfrac32$ are interventions on the solver, not physical collision models.
\item \textbf{Mixed integrators.} Tests A--F, I, J, L, M and the formal pairs use the fixed-step runner. Only the admissible link points were rerun stage-correct. The stage fix moves thresholds by $\lesssim4\times10^{-5}$, below every bracket of Tests A--D.
\item \textbf{The link rate is not converged}, and its dependence on start time is untested.
\item \textbf{The door} is one engineered amplitude on one profile and one history (one cell also run at four amplitudes, Section~\ref{sec:door}), with missing per-run logs and an unexplained $2.85\times10^{-5}$ offset of its unperturbed restart.
\item \textbf{The door's size depends on the collision model.} Letting the single-group rate follow the local neutrino state (a gray port of McCormick's own multigroup rate laws) lowers the threshold by $2.1$--$2.9\times10^{-3}$ and cuts the $\ell=3$ door response by $37\%$ ($2.38$ against $3.76\times10^{-3}$ per unit amplitude). Both are as large as the door itself \grade{measured; one cell; the $37\%$ was not pre-registered}. The abundance estimates of Section~\ref{sec:physics} were not recomputed for it.
\item \textbf{Pre-registration} is self-dated for all but two tests (the angular-resolution Test~M and the $r=0.8$ half of the third grid level, which git proves).
\item \textbf{$\delta_c$, not $C_l$.} McCormick sets abundance with the linear compaction. We report the compaction peak $\delta_c$ throughout; the abundance numbers of Section~\ref{sec:physics} are first estimates.
\item \textbf{Mass.} The black-hole mass is a fraction of the horizon mass that is not computed here (Appendix~\ref{app:massv11}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Two tests posed in the author's frame (not run)}\label{sec:posed}

Both tests were written down, with predictions and kill lines, before any run, in the files \texttt{prereg/PREREG\_RECURRENCE\_v11.txt} and \texttt{prereg/PREREG\_WALL\_IQGT\_v11.txt} (self-dated, 19 November 2026; not committed to git). Neither has been run. Each is stated here so that it can be run as written.

\paragraph{Test R: a record carried over from a previous cycle.} The author's question asks whether hidden structure carried in from a previous relaxation moves the threshold. In the single-rate chain a hidden moment present at $\tau_s$ is erased by $\exp[-\epsilon_{\rm dec}^n/(n\tau_s^n)]$; for $n=3$, $\epsilon_{\rm dec}=2$ this is $0.069$ at $\tau_s=1$, $5.4\times10^{-10}$ at $0.5$ and $7.6\times10^{-75}$ at $0.25$ \grade{computed}. A record that crosses the whole coupled era therefore arrives at decoupling with zero hidden amplitude in the linear chain. The one carrier the model leaves open is the non-linear channel of the general-relativistic solver that already carries $0.3$--$0.5\%$ of the decoupling effect in compaction from $\epsilon_s=0.5$ (Section~\ref{sec:door}). The test plants $\ell=3$ and the Gramian direction at $f=\pm\tfrac12$ (the positivity ceiling, so no relaxed cycle can leave more) and $\pm\tfrac14$, at $\epsilon_s=0.25$ and $0.5$, with an $f=0$ restart at each surface. Prediction (erasure): every pair within two bracket widths. Author's reading: a resolved difference with the decoupling sign ($+p_3$ raises $\delta_c$). Kill lines: erasure dies if any pair differs by more than two bracket widths and survives the drift rule of Section~\ref{sec:door}; the reading dies if every pair is within two widths at $f=\pm\tfrac12$. For scale, the $\epsilon_s=2$, $\ell=3$ response is $3.744\times10^{-3}$ per unit $f$, so a pair there is resolved above $f\approx4.9\times10^{-3}$ on brackets alone and above $f\approx0.091$ under the drift rule \grade{computed from the door table}. \emph{Why not run:} about ten threshold searches of about 15 minutes each on this computer (about $75$\,s per trial, about twelve trials per search), so about $150$ CPU-minutes plus setting up the restart driver, over the budget for this version.

\paragraph{Test W: the wall with the author's tools.} The ruler verdict of Section~\ref{sec:link} rests on a single-vector reading, because the least-damped Riesz projector has no isolated eigenvalue past the onset $\kappa=2k/\pi$ for about half the profile. The author's rule~\cite{IQGT} is to read through the projector of a cluster held by a gap (the tail), not a single eigenvector (the tip). Test~W reads the same fifteen thresholds through (i)~the trace form $q(V)=\tfrac12\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Tr}\,V^2$, $V=d\Pi/dr$, with its sign recorded; (ii)~the contour Riesz projector of the smallest least-damped cluster held by a real-part gap of at least $0.05$ (rank~2 where the least-damped pair is complex); (iii)~the tip and tail gaps logged at every $(k,r)$, the ruler built on the tail only; and (iv)~each segment classified as fold or polar stratum~\cite[Prop.~8.6]{IQGT}. The tools are checked in Lean: the tip/tail gap identity (finite Davis--Kahan, \texttt{gap\_identity}), the freedom of the tip at a tie (\texttt{tip\_free\_at\_tie}) and the trace identities behind $q$ (\texttt{trace\_remark\_2\_3}, \texttt{two\_trace\_PVV}) in \texttt{dicipler-pixel/iqgt-grassmannian-lean}, commit \texttt{1d0af1c}; positivity of the metric on projector tangents and the curvature bound $\Omega^2\le4\det g$ (\texttt{metric\_pos}, \texttt{curvature\_sq\_le}) in \texttt{dicipler-pixel/iqgt-core-lean}, commit \texttt{6160eb4}. Kill lines are those of the original ruler test (family slope ratio in $[0.97,1.03]$; odd-rate spread below $1.2\%$), with a new control: at least $90\%$ of the profile weight must have a gap-held cluster. \emph{Why not run:} the compute is small, but (ii) and (iv) are new code that must be pinned to the definitions in~\cite{IQGT} and checked before a verdict means anything, an estimated one to two hours of building, over the budget for this version.

\section{Readings from \emph{Beyond the Event Horizon}}\label{sec:bhreadings}

The author's black-hole work~\cite{BH} studies what survives when a transport operator reaches a wall of its configuration space. Four of its results meet the neutrino results here. Each connection below is a reading, not a result of this paper \grade{reading}.

\paragraph{The rigidity wall.} The statements used below are machine-checked in \texttt{dicipler-pixel/event-horizon-lean}, commit \texttt{7dd6164}. \cite{BH} sorts walls into two kinds: spectral walls, where a real pair turns complex and a Jordan block appears in every frame, and geometric walls, where the spectrum stays real and the block is an effect of the chart; ``a black hole carries one of each.'' On the regular stratum of the four-body wall the transport spectrum is real (\texttt{spectrum\_real}); at the wall the block is triangular and, under a joint condition, a genuine Jordan block (\texttt{wall\_triangular}, \texttt{wall\_jordan}). The neutrino chain has a spectral wall in its two-moment truncation, the exceptional point at $\kappa=2k/\sqrt3$, and a different wall in the full chain, the onset $\kappa=2k/\pi$, where the kept pole meets the branch cut and its non-orthogonality $4\phi^2/\sin^22\phi$ diverges (Section~\ref{sec:cut}). That the truncated exceptional point is not the onset is this paper's result; which kind of wall the onset is, in the sense of~\cite{BH}, is open, and Test~W is built to decide it.

\paragraph{The fold.} In~\cite{BH} the coplanar wall is a fold: an observer who reads the even quantity sees a bounce and a blow-up, while in the signed coordinate the path passes through, at finite distance, to the mirror configuration. Read cosmologically, this is the author's ``way beyond big bang'': in the radiation era $a\propto\eta$, so the scale factor is the signed coordinate and the even observer is the one who sees a first moment~\cite{BFT}. The neutrino results here do not test this; they supply what a record must be to cross such a fold.

\paragraph{One-way transport.} At the wall of~\cite{BH} the back-coupling vanishes: ``what happens in the wall cannot write back across it, while what arrives across the wall is written into it'' (the back-coupling $A_{21}$ vanishes at $w_3=0$: \texttt{wall\_triangular}). The neutrino cut behaves this way in time. Collisions conserve the kept population and damp the rest, and the kept population obeys $(\dot A)^2\le4E_BA(1-A)$ (Section~\ref{sec:witness}); hidden structure planted in the coupled era is erased, while structure arriving at decoupling is written into the threshold (Section~\ref{sec:door}).

\paragraph{The orientation record.} \cite{BH} has two survival principles: ``the contour keeps the ledger'' and ``the orientation keeps the trace''. Across a mirror, odd readings cancel and even readings survive; a polar stratum reflects totally, $\Gamma=1$ (\texttt{polar\_mirror}), and a step of the projector metric reflects with $\Gamma=\tanh(\tfrac12\ln(\Delta_2/\Delta_1))$ (\texttt{gap\_form}), the same $\tanh$ of half a log-ratio as the link coordinate $(r-1)/(r+1)=\tanh(\tfrac12\ln r)$ of Section~\ref{sec:link} (\texttt{tanh\_half\_log}). The door shows the same bookkeeping: the threshold shift is close to odd in the amplitude, with its sign set by the orientation of the hidden record, and a record of random orientation does not average away but leaves an even remainder that raises the abundance (Section~\ref{sec:physics}). In this reading the orientation of the neutrino record is the trace that survives, and its even part is what a cycle that forgets orientation would still pass on. The boundary compression of~\cite{BH}, ``rank compression, not destruction'', is the exact elimination of the hidden moments in Appendix~A.5, read the same way.

\paragraph{The ledger outlives the metric.} \texttt{dicipler-pixel/gravity-ledger-lean}, commit \texttt{885932f} (30 theorems), proves that every tangent to an idempotent is off-diagonal, that a moving signed metric has an indefinite side (\texttt{moving\_metric\_indefinite\_side}), and that the overlap of equal-trace idempotents has an exact quadratic limit (\texttt{overlap\_quadratic\_limit}). In the frame of Section~\ref{sec:frame} this is the finite algebra behind ``the ledger outlives the metric''~\cite{Ledger}: the overlap of the kept projector stays controlled where a metric reading changes sign.

\paragraph{The square case.} \texttt{dicipler-pixel/square-case-lean}, commit \texttt{6769949} (63 theorems), the Lean companion of~\cite{SquareCase}, proves that the intrinsic flow keeps a tangent on a binary-collision wall (\texttt{intrinsic\_preserves\_wall\_tangent}) and that for orthogonal projections of equal rank $k$, $\mathrm{Tr}(PQ)=k-\tfrac12\|P-Q\|_F^2$. The first is the wall-side half of one-way transport (what is in the wall stays in it); the second is the overlap used when the kept sector is compared across the cut.

\paragraph{The three-body fold.} \texttt{dicipler-pixel/three-body-lean}, commit \texttt{1f3ddcd}, the Lean companion of~\cite{ThreeBody}, proves the fold $\lambda_{\min}(\pi/2+\varepsilon)=\sin^2\varepsilon$ with $\lambda_{\min}/\varepsilon^2\to1$ (\texttt{fold\_lambda}, \texttt{fold\_quadratic}) and the shifted ledger minimum (\texttt{ledger\_min}): the same quadratic touch as the fold of~\cite{BH}.
