\subsection*{D.1 Mass at horizon entry}\label{app:massv11}
In radiation domination $a\propto1/T$ and $H\propto T^2$, so $aH\propto T$ and $\epsilon=1/(aHr_m)\propto1/T$ at fixed comoving $r_m$. If $\epsilon=\epsilon_{\rm dec}$ at $T_{\rm dec}$, then $\epsilon=1$ at $T_{\rm entry}=\epsilon_{\rm dec}T_{\rm dec}$. The horizon mass $M_H=\tfrac{4\pi}{3}\rho H^{-3}c^3\propto T^{-2}$, so $M_H(T_{\rm entry})=M_H(T_{\rm dec})/\epsilon_{\rm dec}^2$. With $\rho=(\pi^2/30)g_*T^4$ and $g_*=10.75$, $M_H(1\,{\rm MeV})=1.50\times10^5\,M_\odot$. For $\epsilon_{\rm dec}=0.5,1,2,3,4,8$ this gives $6.0\times10^5$, $1.5\times10^5$, $3.7\times10^4$, $1.7\times10^4$, $9.4\times10^3$ and $2.3\times10^3\,M_\odot$. A larger $\epsilon_{\rm dec}$ means the scale entered earlier, so it is lighter. The black-hole mass is a fraction of the horizon mass that is not computed here.
